A little bit about me

I am currently the Assistant to the Associate Dean at the UGA Graduate School.I appreciate being able to continue helping the student population. I also value being able to serve on a super diverse and welcoming staff.

I have a Master's degree in Plant Biology and a Bachelor's degree in Biology. My background is in herbarium curation, information science, and plant conservation research. Previously, my specialties have included: information retrieval, reference research, herbarium and field collection, botanical phylogenetics, and conservation genetics. In the past, I have also dabbled in special collection/archival work and species distribution modeling through R, Rstudio, QGIS, and ARC GIS software systems.

I am a National Science Foundation Graduate Research Fellow (NSF GRFP) and a Georgia Research Education Award Fellow (GREAT). I am also an LSAMP (Louis Stokes Alliance for Minority Participation) scholar.

M.S. Graduate Research

My graduate research focused on the phylogenetics of the Echinacea genus and my experiences surrounding being a Black botanist in the Southern United States. To accomplish this research, I used a mix of field collections and herbarium collections. I prepared, mounted, and cataloged all field collections in the UGA Herbarium. All genetic data has been uploaded as accessions to the National Center for Biotechnology Information (NCBI) database.

Phylogenetics

It has been difficult to resolve relationships among many important lineages within the *Asteraceae* due to interspecific hybridization and rapid species diversification throughout the history of the family. Previous efforts to resolve evolutionary relationships among Echinacea species have relied heavily on variation in the plastid genome with limited analysis of nuclear loci. In this research, I combined whole plastome sequences and nuclear gene capture data to reconstruct species relationships and characterize the pace of diversification across the genus. My published work surrounding this topic can be found in the next section.

Black and Brown Experiences

Ensuring diversity within STEM fields requires a focus on understanding how certain identities are marginalized in science and society and how different power and privilege dynamics intersect to create opportunities or barriers for people from different backgrounds. In this research, I discuss some of the experiences surrounding African American graduate students, particularly, literature surrounding this topic and experiences that have impacted me and my colleagues in the field of Plant Biology and Ecology. **These experiences include: a)** Lack of social safety and isolation for African American students at a predominantly white institution (PWI) and b) Issues for African American students regarding field safety and field work in the rural south
